# The *Quaestio* Template

The structure below is the article form of Aquinas' *Summa Theologiae* (c. 1265–1274),
which runs to several thousand articles all built to this same five-part shape.

## Full form

```
UTRUM — Whether [proposition, as a strict yes/no].

VIDETUR QUOD — It seems that [the position you will reject], because:
  1. [Objection, in its strongest form]
  2. [Objection, independent of the first]
  3. [Objection, independent of both]

SED CONTRA — But on the contrary: [the one consideration
  the objections do not account for].

RESPONDEO — I answer that [determination].
  [The distinction it rests on: the term that was doing two
   jobs, true in one sense, false in the other.]

AD PRIMUM — To the first: [concede / deny / distinguish], because…
AD SECUNDUM — To the second: [concede / deny / distinguish], because…
AD TERTIUM — To the third: [concede / deny / distinguish], because…
```

## Compressed form, for a chat reply

All five parts survive; only the ceremony goes.

```
The question is whether X or Y.

The case for Y — which is stronger than it gets credit for:
  · [objection]
  · [objection]

What decides it: [the consideration they don't account for].

X. [Determination, with the distinction if there is one.]

On the first — I concede that, and accept [cost].
On the second — I deny it: [the premise that fails].
```

## Notes on each part

### *Utrum*

**A strict binary, or the question is not ready.** You cannot state objections to a topic,
only to a proposition. If the binary feels forced, that is diagnostic: the terms are still
equivocal and the question needs dissolving before it can be decided.

This step fails more often than any other, and its failures are unrecoverable — a false
dilemma or a smuggled premise here is inherited by every step after it.

### *Videtur quod* — the objections

Arguments **for the position you will reject**, conventionally three. This inversion is the
whole method: Aquinas opens every article by arguing against himself, and argues well
enough that his objections are still quoted by people who hold those views.

- **Strongest form, not convenient form** — the objection an intelligent proponent would
  actually raise
- **In their terms** — no pre-loaded qualifiers, no tone that signals it is about to lose
- **Separately answerable** — three restatements of one objection is one objection

### *Sed contra*

One decisive consideration that halts the momentum the objections have built. In Aquinas
it is usually an authority, deployed as a pivot rather than as proof. The modern analogue
is the hard fact, measurement, or constraint the objections have not accounted for.

### *Respondeo* — the determination

The characteristic move is the **distinction**. A good *respondeo* usually does not choose
between the objections and the thesis; it finds a term both sides were using in one sense,
splits it into two, and shows the contradiction was equivocation. True *in one respect*,
false in another — *secundum quid*.

**A *respondeo* that only picks a winner has not used the method.** It has applied a
scoring rubric. Find the distinction, or establish that there genuinely isn't one and the
objections are simply wrong.

### The replies

**The step that gets skipped**, and skipping it is what makes a correct conclusion feel
slippery. Each objection gets its own reply, in order.

The canonical pattern: **concede as much as possible, then locate the exact point of
failure.** Aquinas' replies characteristically run *"this would be true if X, but X holds
only in a certain respect…"* — granting the objection's force within its proper scope,
denying only the overreach.

A reply that restates the *respondeo* is not a reply. If your answer to the second
objection is "as explained above," it was never engaged.

## The three declared moves

In live disputation the *respondens* could not deflect, expand, or change the subject.
Each objection had to be met with one of three:

- ***Concedo*** — I grant it, and I live with the consequence
- ***Nego*** — I deny it, and here is the premise I reject
- ***Distinguo*** — I distinguish: true in this sense, false in that one

**Being required to declare which move you are making is the entire discipline.** It
eliminates the whole family of evasions — the sympathetic restatement, the pivot to an
adjacent point, "great question, and also…" — because none of them can be labelled.
